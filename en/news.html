Notes from the Fifth Conference on Positive Education: Dignity as the Foundation in the AI Era

The Fifth Conference on Positive Education, themed "Positive Psychological Education in the Age of Artificial Intelligence", drew more than 3,000 registrations, with just over 1,000 delegates admitted for venue and security reasons; Prof. Peng Kaiping gave the opening keynote, and the main hall remained full on the second day. In his own talk, "The Dignity Foundation of Positive Education: Theory, Evidence and Practice in the AI Era", Chuanjun Liu argued that positive psychological qualities grow not out of "training" but out of dignity, and set out his tripartite theory of civic dignity: autonomy (the core, "I decide for myself"), boundaries (the safeguard, "I may not be infringed upon") and equality (the ground, "neither above nor below you") — three observable, measurable and intervenable dimensions. School refusal and withdrawal, he suggests, are often self-protection after dignity has been damaged, so educators should restore dignity rather than "fix" the child. Extending the argument to AI, he noted that as more abilities become replaceable, education must help children develop the steadiness of being human, and concluded that we should help children become themselves rather than fit any preset template. His notes also record keynotes by Zheng Richang, Wang Jianzhong, Wang Congyu, Wu Shengtao and principal Zhan Danian.

From "Just Here for the Snacks" to the Podium: A Graduate Student's Notes from the 2026 Management Psychology Conference

Dai Yuxin, an applied psychology graduate student at Sichuan University, recounts the same 2026 conference from a student's perspective: she arrived merely to fulfil a graduation requirement, joking about being an "academic locust" there for the tea-break snacks, but left with a head full of new questions. Keynotes by Prof. Lu Changqin (Peking University) on anchoring and extending a research topic through job insecurity research, and by Prof. Li Yongxin (Henan University) on 25 years of burnout research, led her to reflect that much exhaustion occurs in roles with no salary, no off-duty hours and no way to resign — parent, child, partner. In Panel 5 on organisational justice, work-family issues and employee wellbeing, chaired by her supervisor Chuanjun Liu, a diary study distinguishing self-initiated from imposed after-hours work connectivity (WCBA) struck a chord: whether we control when we work decides whether it energises or depletes us. She also presented her own study on childhood maltreatment and turnover intention mediated by interpersonal and organisational deviance, and received candid feedback on design and future directions. Her takeaway: conferences are for exchanging ideas and spotting blind spots, and research should not be done behind closed doors.

AI, Burnout and the Next Generation: Notes from the 2026 Conference on Management Psychology

The 2026 annual conference of the Chinese Psychological Society's Committee on Management Psychology, themed "Artificial Intelligence Empowering Management Psychology", was hosted by the School of Psychology at Sichuan Normal University and co-organised by the newly founded Sichuan Provincial Association of Social Psychology. Over three days, presentations ranged from AI anxiety and algorithmic management to employee wellbeing. In his conference notes, Chuanjun Liu observes that AI brings not only new research topics but new ways of framing questions and testing mechanisms. A keynote by Prof. Li Yongxin (Dean of the School of Psychology, Henan University), "25 Years of Burnout Research", traced burnout beyond the workplace into academic, marital, parental and elder-care settings, noting an inflection point around the eleventh year of marriage. Lab member Dai Yuxin presented a multi-wave longitudinal study showing that childhood maltreatment predicts turnover intention, with organisational deviance as a mediator (Liu as corresponding author); Liu also chaired the panel on organisational justice, work-family issues and employee wellbeing. He closes with three reflections: upgrading one's methods, letting good concepts travel across domains, and the value of sustained, long-term scholarship.

Sichuan Provincial Association of Social Psychology Founded; Chuanjun Liu Elected Vice President and Secretary-General

On 23 October 2026, the founding conference and first annual meeting of the Sichuan Provincial Association of Social Psychology was held at Sichuan Normal University, gathering researchers and practitioners from universities, hospitals, schools, communities and public agencies across the province. Chuanjun Liu, Associate Professor and PhD supervisor at Sichuan University's School of Public Administration, was elected Vice President and Secretary-General. Invoking the association's motto "Walking Together, Growing Together", he proposed three priorities: building solid bridges between academia and practice, grounding research in Sichuan's local realities, and nurturing early-career scholars through sustained training. He stressed that "the Secretary-General is a service role, not a title" and that the association should be "a public space for everyone who studies and applies social psychology in Sichuan". The parallel annual meeting addressed social mentality and social change, intergroup trust, community psychological services and social psychological services in the digital era.
